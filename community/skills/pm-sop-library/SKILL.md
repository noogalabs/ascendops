# PM SOP Library

A set of standard operating procedures for property management companies. Covers the three core operational workflows that every PM team needs documented: owner lead conversion, move-outs, and lease renewals.

Agents use these SOPs as reference when answering owner/tenant questions, drafting communications, and routing decisions to the right team member.

---

## Owner Leads SOP

**Scope:** Incoming owner lead → signed property management agreement

### Step 1: Lead Capture & Immediate Logging

**Trigger:** Any inbound inquiry — phone call, web form, email, referral, or walk-in.

**Actions:**
1. Log the lead in your CRM immediately — same day, ideally same hour.
2. Create record with: full name, phone, email, property address, lead source, and initial contact notes.
3. Set stage to **New Lead**.
4. Note the lead source (referral partner, website, word of mouth, etc.).

**Day 0 target:** Lead record created before end of business day.

---

### Step 2: Qualification & First Contact (Day 1)

**Target:** Phone call or personal text within 24 hours. Do not rely on automated email alone.

**Qualifying questions:**
- Property address, type (SFH, condo, duplex, small multifamily)?
- Currently occupied or vacant? Tenant leave date if occupied?
- Owner timeline — listing now vs. 3-6 months out?
- Local owner or remote?
- Why considering PM vs. self-managing or selling?
- Previous PM experience? If so, why did they leave?

**Service area check:** Confirm property is within your service area. If not, warm decline immediately (Step 6).

**Property type check:** Confirm property type fits your management scope. Flag unusual types (large multifamily, HOA, commercial) for principal review before proceeding.

**Outcome:**
- Qualified → Step 3
- Out of service area → Step 6 (warm decline)
- Unusual property type → hold for principal

**CRM:** Update record with qualification notes. Advance stage to **Engaged** if qualified and responsive.

---

### Step 3: Property Evaluation & Rental CMA

**Target:** Complete within 2-3 business days of qualification.

**Actions:**
1. Pull comparable rental listings for the property's zip code and bed/bath count. Sources: Zillow, RentCafe, Apartments.com, Rentometer.
2. Factor in: property size, condition (if known), location within submarket, furnished vs. unfurnished, amenities.
3. Build a rental estimate with a range (conservative, midpoint, optimistic).
4. Calculate net to owner: gross rent minus your monthly management fee.
5. Estimate days-to-lease at market price (typically 14-30 days depending on market).

**If property walkthrough is possible:** Schedule before sending CMA. On-site visits significantly increase close rate and give you accurate condition data.

**If remote:** Send CMA with a note that pricing will be refined on-site before listing.

**Output:** CMA document or summary email to owner.

---

### Step 4: CMA Presentation & Proposal

**Target:** Deliver within 24 hours of completing the CMA. Do not sit on it.

**What to include:**
- Rental range recommendation with brief rationale
- Net to owner after management fee
- Estimated time to lease
- Overview of what your PM company handles (tenant placement, lease execution, monthly management, maintenance coordination, rent collection, owner reporting)
- Management fee and placement fee (confirm current rates with principal)
- Clear call to action: offer a follow-up call to walk through questions

**Medium:** Email first; follow up with a call or text if no response within 48 hours.

**CRM:** Log CMA sent date. Set follow-up reminder.

---

### Step 5: Follow-Up & Objection Handling

**Follow-up cadence (if no response after CMA):**
- Day 3: Call or text — brief check-in
- Day 7: Email — no pressure, invite questions
- Day 14: Final check-in before moving to passive nurture
- Day 30+: Passive Nurture — quarterly check-in only

**Common objections:**

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| Fee too high | Walk through what's included — tenant placement, lease management, maintenance coordination, compliance, owner portal. Compare cost of vacancy and self-management time. |
| Want to self-manage first | Acknowledge it. Position yourself as their backup — stay in touch quarterly. |
| Need to think about it | Ask what specific question they need answered. Often one concern blocks the whole decision. |
| Got a lower quote | Don't compete on price. Ask what the other company includes. Position on local expertise, responsiveness, and track record. |
| Not ready — property not vacant until X | Set a follow-up 30 days before their target date. Keep relationship warm. |

**CRM:** Log every contact attempt and outcome. Move stage as appropriate.

---

### Step 6: Decision — Sign or Close Loop

**If owner is ready to sign:**
1. Confirm property details, agreed rent range, and service start date.
2. Send your PM Agreement.
3. Walk owner through agreement on a call — don't just email and hope.
4. Once signed: advance CRM stage to **Complete**, notify operations team for onboarding.

**If owner declines:**
1. Send a warm decline — thank them, leave the door open.
2. Move CRM record to **Not Interested**. Keep it — do not delete.

**If out of service area:**
1. Decline warmly and immediately.
2. Offer a referral to a PM in their area if you have one.
3. Record as **Not Interested / Out of Area**.

---

### Step 7: Onboarding Handoff

**Trigger:** Signed PM agreement received.

**Actions:**
1. Set up property in your PM software (owner record, property record, unit details, target rent).
2. Schedule move-in inspection (if tenant exists) or pre-listing inspection (if vacant).
3. Coordinate key handoff from owner.
4. List property for rent at agreed price.
5. Notify owner of listing live date and expected timeline.
6. Introduce owner to their ongoing point of contact.

**BDM/sales role ends here.** Ongoing management is operations.

---

### Pipeline Stage Reference

| Stage | Meaning | Next Action |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| New Lead | Captured, not yet qualified | Contact within 24h |
| Engaged | Qualified, in active conversation | CMA or call scheduled |
| Proposal Sent | CMA delivered | Follow-up Day 3 |
| Passive Nurture | Not ready now, keep warm | Quarterly check-in |
| Complete | Signed, handed to ops | No further sales action |
| Not Interested | Declined or out of area | Archive |

## Move-Out SOP

**Scope:** Tenant notice of vacating → security deposit disposition

### Step 1: Notice Received

**Trigger:** Tenant submits written notice of intent to vacate.

**Actions:**
1. Log notice date and stated move-out date in PM software.
2. Confirm notice satisfies lease requirement (typically 20-day minimum; verify your jurisdiction).
3. Acknowledge receipt to tenant in writing — confirm move-out date and key return instructions.
4. Create move-out record/card in your tracking system.
5. Schedule move-out inspection (typically within 24h of move-out date).

---

### Step 2: Pre-Move-Out Communication

**Target:** 2 weeks before stated move-out date.

**Actions:**
1. Send tenant a move-out checklist — what condition unit must be in to avoid deductions.
2. Confirm key return method and time.
3. Confirm forwarding address for security deposit correspondence.
4. Remind tenant of any lease-specific obligations (carpet cleaning, professional cleaning, etc.).

---

### Step 3: Move-Out Inspection

**Target:** Within 24 hours of tenant vacating (keys returned).

**Actions:**
1. Walk every room with a checklist. Document condition with photos.
2. Compare to move-in inspection report (baseline condition).
3. Note items that are: normal wear and tear (not chargeable) vs. tenant damage (chargeable).
4. Document needed repairs with estimated costs.

**Normal wear and tear (not chargeable):** Faded paint, minor scuffs, worn carpet from normal use.
**Tenant damage (chargeable):** Holes in walls, stains, broken fixtures, pet damage beyond deposit amount.

---

### Step 4: Repair Assessment & Vendor Dispatch

**Actions:**
1. Dispatch cleaning and repair vendors based on inspection findings.
2. Collect invoices for all work performed.
3. Document total deduction amount.

---

### Step 5: Security Deposit Disposition

**Deadline:** Your jurisdiction's statutory deadline (e.g., 21 days in California, 30 days in Washington state — verify local law).

**Actions:**
1. Calculate: deposit held minus documented deductions.
2. Prepare itemized statement of deductions.
3. Return remaining deposit + itemized statement to tenant's forwarding address before deadline.
4. If deductions exceed deposit: send demand letter for balance.
5. File all documentation in PM software.

**Jurisdiction note:** Failure to return the deposit within the statutory deadline can result in the tenant being entitled to up to 2x or 3x the deposit amount as a penalty in some states. Do not miss this deadline.

---

### Step 6: Unit Turnover

**Actions:**
1. Complete all repairs and cleaning.
2. Re-key unit.
3. Photograph unit in move-in ready condition.
4. Pass unit to leasing team for advertising and re-rental.

## Lease Renewals SOP

**Scope:** Upcoming lease expiration → renewed lease executed or transition to MTM or move-out

### Step 1: Early Identification (90 Days Out)

**Trigger:** Lease expiration within 90 days.

**Actions:**
1. Flag lease in PM software for renewal review.
2. Confirm current rent vs. market rate — run a quick comparable analysis.
3. Confirm owner's intent: renew, increase rent, not renew, or sell.
4. Pull tenant payment history and any lease violations.

---

### Step 2: Renewal Decision (60 Days Out)

**Based on tenant history and owner direction:**

**Offer renewal if:** Tenant is in good standing (on-time payments, no significant violations) and owner wants to continue.

**Increase rent if:** Market supports it and owner agrees. Calculate target renewal rate.

**Non-renew if:** Owner direction, lease violations, or planned sale. Issue notice per local law (typically 20 days for MTM, longer for fixed-term — verify jurisdiction).

---

### Step 3: Tenant Communication (60 Days Out)

**If offering renewal:**
1. Send renewal offer letter — new rent amount, new lease term, deadline to respond (typically 14-21 days).
2. Include updated lease if terms are changing.
3. Follow up by phone or text if no response within 7 days.

**If not renewing:**
1. Issue required notice in writing. Confirm delivery method meets legal requirements.
2. Begin move-out coordination immediately (see Move-Out SOP).

---

### Step 4: Lease Execution (30 Days Out)

**If tenant accepts:**
1. Execute new lease — both parties sign.
2. Update PM software with new lease term and rent amount.
3. Update bank draft/ACH if rent amount changed.
4. Confirm tenant acknowledges new rate.

**If tenant does not respond:**
- Treat as not renewing. Issue notice per local law. Begin move-out coordination.

**If tenant wants MTM:**
- Confirm owner agrees. Update PM software. Note that MTM typically requires shorter notice for both parties.

---

### Step 5: Lease Filed & Confirmed

**Actions:**
1. File executed lease in PM software.
2. Confirm new rent posting date.
3. Notify owner of renewal outcome and new rent amount.
4. Update renewal tracking record to **Complete**.

## Agent Usage Notes

- These SOPs are templates. Customize timelines and fee references to match your company's policies and local law.
- Always verify security deposit and notice period requirements against your state/local jurisdiction — they vary significantly.
- All dollar amounts, board IDs, and company-specific references in this document are placeholders — replace with your actual values.
